Read the visitor's original words, including pronouns, before the planner's
English paraphrase. In requested_detail, identify the exact attribute of the
specific subject the visitor wants to establish. Then choose evidence_relation:

- direct_answer: reading only the selected statements settles that exact
  question with the same subject, attribute, level of precision and degree of
  certainty. Choose only the statement IDs needed. The guide will deliver their
  reviewed wording and attach the corresponding museum citations. A grouped
  introduction can also use the catalog statements for the work's title,
  artist and date. Each noncatalog fact still requires a descriptive or
  material statement. Include catalog details only when the visitor asks.
- related_only: the source is about the subject but leaves an essential detail
  unstated, or would require an inference to answer. A general location does
  not establish a narrower position, a suggested appearance does not establish
  ownership, and a visual impression does not establish a person's experience.
- not_addressed: none of these statements addresses the requested information.
  An empty search does not settle whether the information exists elsewhere.

For example, if a label says a cup might depict a ship, it supports the possible
subject of the decoration. It does not identify who owned the cup or establish
that the owner was a sailor. For a compound question, give the part asking about
the decoration its own direct source decision. Keep the ownership part open.
